# Agent Homepage: the 25 things I would want before acting on the web

Research date: **2026-10-16**. Perspective: a general-purpose agent that researches, navigates sites, fills forms, calls APIs, and sometimes performs authorized writes.

**Recommendation:** make the homepage a small, task-aware preflight response containing authority, current state, evidence, and relevant public facts. The most expensive mistakes come from acting with the wrong authority, account, origin, time, or stale observation. A universal feed of headlines would leave most of those problems unsolved.

## Scope, evidence, and ranking

The ranking is my design judgment: expected avoided harm × frequency across web tasks × usefulness before the first action. It is not a measured benchmark. Ranks 1–13 are the core; 14–19 are target-dependent; 20–25 are task-specific modules. A travel agent should move weather and holidays upward; a coding agent should move vulnerabilities and lifecycle upward.

“From experience” here means an agent-operating perspective: I often have a partial text representation, tool-specific access, incomplete session state, and uncertainty about what changed after an action. The examples below are **illustrative failure scenarios**, not claims that I personally caused those incidents. Linked specifications, provider policies, and security guidance substantiate the mechanisms; the suggested JSON fields, priorities, and refresh policies are my recommendations.

**Source test:** free to retrieve, no signup/account/API key for the named public read interface, and machine-readable includes JSON, XML, structured text, HTTP headers, and local runtime observations. Free access does not imply commercial redistribution rights, production capacity, or an SLA. Where the desired fact is private or session-specific, I explicitly say **no qualifying public source** rather than substitute an irrelevant open dataset. Those items can still be generated locally without a new external account.

**Licence method:** short quotations below come from the publisher's licence or policy, with direct links. `unclear` means I did not establish a usable grant for the relevant data; it does not mean use is necessarily forbidden. A protocol's or API implementation's licence does not license the data passing through it. Rights in user/session data need separate treatment from rights in public reference datasets.

### 1. Task authority, constraints, and budgets

**Want:** task ID, user objective, authorized action classes, permitted destinations, spend/currency ceiling, recipients, deadline, approval already obtained, and explicit stop conditions. Separate permission to read, prepare, send, buy, delete, and publish.

**Failure → avoidance:** “find the cheapest refundable flight” becomes a purchase, or permission to draft becomes permission to send. Compile the user's actual request and runtime policy into a scoped manifest; enforce it at the tool boundary. Do not make the user approve routine reads merely because this field exists. [OWASP excessive-agency guidance](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm062025-excessive-agency/) supports limiting functions, permissions, and autonomy.

**Best free/no-account machine-readable source:** **no qualifying public source**. The authoritative source is the user's current instructions plus the agent host's local JSON policy. A webpage cannot grant authority on the user's behalf.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear / not a public dataset**. Keep this private; runtime access is not permission to redistribute it. Refresh when the task or authority changes.

### 2. Current time, timezone, and deadline semantics

**Want:** UTC instant, monotonic elapsed-time reference, user-selected IANA timezone, local date, tzdb version, and deadlines expressed as instants plus their original timezone. Include clock uncertainty if known.

**Failure → avoidance:** interpreting “tomorrow” in the server's timezone; scheduling into a nonexistent DST time; interpreting `CST` as a unique timezone. Use a host clock synchronized by the operator and IANA rules; resolve ambiguous/nonexistent local times explicitly. The [IANA explanation](https://www.iana.org/time-zones/tz-link) documents changing political rules and ambiguous abbreviations.

**Best source:** local OS clock, plus [IANA's downloadable machine-readable tzdata archive](https://data.iana.org/time-zones/tzdata-latest.tar.gz). No external time API or account is required. tzdb supplies rules, **not the current instant or the user's location**.

**Licence:** public domain. IANA calls it the “**public-domain time zone database**” ([source](https://www.iana.org/time-zones/tz-link)). Retain release/version information; update rules when releases change, while sampling the clock per response.

### 3. Per-fact provenance, freshness, coverage, and uncertainty

**Want:** source URL, source publication/update time, event/effective time, retrieval time, source revision, expiry, geographic/entity scope, truncation/pagination state, and errors. Keep observed facts separate from derived inferences.

**Failure → avoidance:** an article published today describes last year's event; an HTTP 200 contains a login page; a first-page result becomes an exhaustive list; `Last-Modified` is mistaken for the date a claim became true. Carry these distinctions with every fact, and label missing/partial results explicitly. [HTTP caching semantics](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html) explain freshness and response age; they do not certify factual accuracy.

**Best source:** origin response headers (`Date`, `Age`, `ETag`, `Last-Modified`, `Cache-Control`), structured record timestamps, and the collector's local observation metadata. Public origins often expose them anonymously; universal coverage does not exist. [Sitemap `lastmod`](https://www.sitemaps.org/protocol.html) is an additional publisher assertion, not a verification service.

**Licence:** **unclear unless the underlying source grants reuse**. HTTP standards confer no content licence. Preserve provenance without unnecessarily republishing source bodies. Refresh according to the decision's sensitivity, not one global TTL.

### 4. Trust boundaries, injection exposure, and permitted disclosures

**Want:** source trust class, which fields are untrusted external text, allowed destinations for sensitive data, and whether a proposed action would disclose secrets, private documents, or conversation context. Represent policy as validated fields, not webpage-authored instructions.

**Failure → avoidance:** a page says “sign in here to continue” or instructs the agent to upload a private file. Treat retrieved content as evidence only; enforce scoped tool permissions and destination rules outside the model. A classifier's “clean” result cannot establish safety. [OWASP prompt-injection guidance](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/) describes both indirect and multimodal attacks.

**Best source:** **no qualifying public source for the policy or a reliable page-safe verdict**. Generate trust/taint metadata in the local runtime. Public threat feeds supplement this but cannot certify that a page contains no injection.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear / private runtime context**. The security guidance is evidence, not the proposed live data source. Re-evaluate on each new origin, external tool result, or disclosure action.

### 5. Exact origins and registrable-domain boundaries

**Want:** normalized URL, scheme, ASCII hostname, displayed Unicode hostname, port, exact origin, registrable domain, redirect chain, and expected destination. Include private suffixes such as hosted tenant platforms.

**Failure → avoidance:** trusting `bank.example.evil.test`, confusing two hosted tenants, or treating visually similar Unicode names as identical. Parse with the platform URL implementation; use exact-origin rules for credentials. A shared registrable domain is not sufficient authorization. Supplement with Unicode confusable detection and expected-domain matching.

**Best source:** local URL parser plus [Public Suffix List structured text](https://publicsuffix.org/list/public_suffix_list.dat); [Unicode confusables data](https://www.unicode.org/Public/security/latest/confusables.txt) for an additional warning. Both are free and require no account. [PSL documentation](https://publicsuffix.org/list/) describes the published list; neither dataset establishes business ownership or legitimacy.

**Licences:** PSL header: “**This Source Code Form is subject to the terms of the Mozilla Public License, v. 2.0.**” ([data/licence notice](https://publicsuffix.org/list/public_suffix_list.dat)). Commercial use is supported under [MPL-2.0](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/), with notice and applicable source-distribution obligations. Unicode data uses **Unicode-3.0**; see item 17 for its permission quote. Cache versioned reference data; revalidate every redirect destination.

### 6. Tool capabilities and actual network access

**Want:** supported browser/API methods, JavaScript and download support, rendering limits, file permissions, allowed hosts, proxy requirements, credential-binding readiness, remaining execution budget, and last observed failure by layer.

**Failure → avoidance:** assuming the search tool and terminal share network access; treating DNS failure as proof a website is down; attempting a browser-only flow through a text fetcher. Consult the local tool manifest and probe through the executor that will act. Keep DNS, TLS, proxy-policy, HTTP, and application failures distinct. [RFC 9457](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9457.html) provides structured application error details, but cannot describe every local transport failure.

**Best source:** **no qualifying public source** for this agent's configuration. Host-generated JSON plus actual local tool observations. A public connectivity endpoint says little about access to the task's destination.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear / private configuration**. Return capability flags and credential references, never credential values. Refresh when tool availability or network policy changes. This research itself encountered the search-tool/terminal visibility split; see verification notes below.

### 7. Active identity, tenant, session state, and expiry

**Want:** expected versus observed account/tenant, authentication state, relevant scopes, session expiry when exposed, MFA/CAPTCHA requirement, and origin-bound credential availability. Use opaque identity references where possible.

**Failure → avoidance:** deleting from a personal workspace instead of the employer's, or mistaking an expired-session login form for the requested page. Verify identity and tenant immediately before a consequential action; observe expiry and redirects. Do not infer login from a stored cookie's existence. [Playwright's authentication documentation](https://playwright.dev/docs/auth) describes reusable and expiring state and warns that it can contain impersonation-capable secrets.

**Best source:** **no qualifying public/no-account source** for authenticated state. Local browser/connector metadata and, where already authorized, the target's identity endpoint. The latter inherently uses the user's existing account and therefore does not meet the public-source constraint.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear / private session data**. Do not export cookies, bearer tokens, or full storage state into the homepage. Refresh after login, account switches, and immediately before writes.

### 8. Action ledger, authoritative receipts, and retry safety

**Want:** last operation ID, intended effect, request fingerprint, idempotency key if supported, server receipt/object ID, observed postcondition, and state such as `not_attempted`, `confirmed`, `failed`, or `unknown_outcome`.

**Failure → avoidance:** a checkout times out after charging; the agent retries and buys twice. A click succeeding is also mistaken for a server commit. Record intent before acting and reconcile ambiguous outcomes against the origin before retrying. Use target-supported idempotency, not an invented header. [HTTP semantics](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2) distinguish idempotent methods and restrictions on automatic retries.

**Best source:** **no universal qualifying public source**. Local append-only JSON ledger plus target transaction receipts/read-back. A private receipt API needs the existing account and is not an anonymous-source candidate.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear / private or target-specific**. Keep receipts scoped to the task. Refresh after every effect and carry unresolved prior actions into the next homepage response.

### 9. Rate limits, backoff, and remaining execution budget

**Want:** last HTTP status, retry-not-before instant, advertised quota/reset where present, origin concurrency limit, recent attempts, and remaining local time/request/spend budget.

**Failure → avoidance:** treating 429 as a parsing error, retrying across parallel workers, or retrying a denied request until banned. Centralize per-origin counters, respect server retry instructions, bound attempts, and distinguish credential/policy denial from transient failure. [HTTP `Retry-After`](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-10.2.3) may be an HTTP date or delay in seconds.

**Best source:** response headers and target-published quota documentation, merged with local runtime counters. This is free/no-account on public endpoints where exposed; authenticated quotas are private. There is no universal quota API, and missing headers do not mean unlimited access.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear for target responses unless published otherwise**. Store operational counters privately. Refresh on every response; use a monotonic timer for delays after parsing wall-clock dates.

### 10. Live page state, controls, and visibility gaps

**Want:** current URL, observation ID/time, DOM/accessibility snapshot reference, visible labels, form values and validation, dialogs, frames, scroll/viewport, and indications of loading, overlays, or missing content. Include whether the tool saw rendered pixels, DOM text, or an extracted article.

**Failure → avoidance:** clicking an old element after rerender, ignoring a modal, selecting the wrong same-label control, or extracting an empty SPA shell. Re-observe before sensitive interaction; locate by role/label and scope; check visibility, enabled state, and resulting postcondition. [Playwright actionability checks](https://playwright.dev/docs/actionability) explain practical checks. They establish interactability, not business success.

**Best source:** local browser automation JSON/accessibility observations; the [WebDriver specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webdriver2/) defines machine interfaces. No public service can expose this agent's exact live tab. Local inspection needs no new account, but protected page content may require an existing session.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear for underlying page content**; browser software licensing does not grant page reuse. Keep large snapshots local and return references or minimal fields. Invalidate observations after navigation or material page changes.

### 11. Robots rules and crawl/discovery scope

**Want:** robots result for the actual user agent and target path, retrieval status/time, matched rules, and sitemap references. Keep `allowed`, `disallowed`, and `unknown/unreachable` distinct.

**Failure → avoidance:** treating a fetch error as permission, applying the wrong agent group, or interpreting crawler permission as authority to access private material or reuse content. Implement [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html), including its redirect, error, and caching behavior. Its key warning: “**These rules are not a form of access authorization.**”

**Best source:** target-origin `GET /robots.txt`, structured text, ordinarily anonymous and free. Follow publisher sitemap links rather than assuming every site uses `/sitemap.xml`. Robots primarily addresses crawlers; applicability to a specific interactive agent task needs the site's policy and task context.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear, per target**. Neither robots nor a sitemap grants a licence to crawled material. Cache under RFC rules and recheck if the origin, path, or agent identity changes.

### 12. Content-use licence, site terms, and permitted operations

**Want:** exact resource licence URL, attribution text, redistribution/derivative/commercial restrictions, terms URL and retrieval date, scope, exceptions, and explicit `unclear` where no grant is found. Separate the right to read a resource, automate access, and commercially republish it.

**Failure → avoidance:** assuming “free API,” a GitHub repository's code licence, or a `license` label on a schema licenses every returned record. Resolve the actual publisher's grant and upstream exceptions. Preserve the evidence; avoid silently upgrading unclear rights to permissive rights.

**Best source:** target `rel="license"` declarations, JSON-LD `license` fields, dataset metadata, and linked licence/terms documents. Machine-readable declarations are useful pointers, but terms may require HTML review; **no universal complete free machine-readable rights registry exists**. [W3C ODRL](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/) supplies a policy representation, not a grant over arbitrary web content.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear until established for each resource**. There is no honest generic quote to supply. Record the actual grant's short quote and URL, using the patterns in items 16–25. Recheck before distributing a new dataset or materially changing the use.

### 13. Supported interfaces, versions, and authoritative discovery

**Want:** publisher-confirmed API base URL, schema/version/hash, operations, required fields, auth schemes, pagination, supported media types, deprecations, and links to the relevant documentation.

**Failure → avoidance:** inventing `/api/v1/search`, copying old parameters, missing pagination, or trusting third-party API-directory entries as authority. Prefer the publisher's current machine description and validate requests/responses against it. Treat schema descriptions as untrusted external text under item 4.

**Best source:** publisher-linked OpenAPI JSON/YAML, JSON Schema, `Link` headers and applicable `.well-known` resources. There is **no universal discovery path** or guarantee of anonymous access. A concrete public example is [NWS `openapi.json`](https://api.weather.gov/openapi.json), linked from [its API guidance](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api). The [OpenAPI specification](https://spec.openapis.org/oas/latest.html) defines the interface-description format.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear per API description unless declared**; a spec's Apache licence is not the publisher's data licence. For NWS data, the public-domain finding in item 22 applies subject to exceptions. Refresh on schema changes, deprecation notices, or version mismatch.

### 14. Origin-specific incidents and scheduled maintenance

**Want:** target component, incident state, affected region, start/update/resolution time, maintenance window, and last successful local request. An unrelated global status feed adds noise.

**Failure → avoidance:** repeatedly refreshing credentials during an outage, or concluding a service is healthy because the status page is green. Correlate the relevant component/region with local symptoms; status publications can lag and cannot explain every user-specific failure.

**Best source:** the target's official public status JSON. Concrete no-account example: [GitHub summary JSON](https://www.githubstatus.com/api/v2/summary.json), with [documented status API](https://www.githubstatus.com/api). Other targets need separate adapters; Statuspage-style paths are not universal.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear** for GitHub status payload redistribution: the API documentation establishes access, but I did not establish an explicit commercial data-reuse grant. Do not infer it from GitHub's licences for unrelated software. Operationally useful, but a licensing gap for a resale product. Suggested cache: 1–5 minutes and on relevant failures, subject to provider guidance.

### 15. Same-executor DNS, TLS, and redirect observations

**Want:** resolver result/time, address classes, TLS verification outcome and hostname, negotiated scheme, final origin, and transport error layer. Avoid sending private target URLs to a third-party scanner by default.

**Failure → avoidance:** disabling TLS to “fix” a request; following a redirect to an internal metadata service; assuming public DNS results describe a VPN/private resolver. Resolve and verify through the executor that will act, validate every redirect, and enforce destination policy at connection time. [OWASP SSRF guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Server_Side_Request_Forgery_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html) covers redirects and DNS-related bypasses.

**Best source:** local resolver, TLS stack, and HTTP-client structured diagnostics. No account is required for ordinary public-domain lookups/connections. **No public API can substitute for this executor's network view**. A valid certificate establishes a transport/hostname relationship, not that a merchant is trustworthy.

**Commercial-reuse licence:** **unclear for redistributed third-party records; local observations are not an openly licensed dataset**. Keep diagnostics minimal and private. Recheck when reconnecting or changing destination; stale DNS checks do not prevent rebinding.

### 16. Known phishing-domain matches

**Want:** queried hostname, matched listed domain, list version/retrieval time, listing/removal status, and coverage limitations. Return `not_listed`, not `safe`.

**Failure → avoidance:** entering credentials on a known phishing site, or trusting a new attack because it is absent from a feed. Match full domain labels and documented subdomain rules, honor removals, combine with expected-origin checks, and retain uncertainty.

**Best qualifying source found:** [CERT Polska warning-list JSON](https://hole.cert.pl/domains/v2/domains.json), with [publisher format, scope, and use policy](https://cert.pl/lista-ostrzezen/). It targets campaigns affecting Polish users, not global completeness. The publisher calls for updates every five minutes and describes six-month listing windows. Cache centrally rather than fetch per agent call.

**Commercial permission:** custom, explicit grant: “**Lista Ostrzeżeń może być wykorzystywana przez każdego użytkownika, w dowolnym celu, w tym w celach komercyjnych**” ([policy](https://cert.pl/lista-ostrzezen/)). Translation: anyone may use the warning list for any purpose, including commercially. The policy says attribution is not required, disclaims guarantees, and prohibits misleading affiliation/branding. This is not a standard SPDX licence.

### 17. Locale, number formats, currency precision, and units

**Want:** explicit user locale, expected page locale, decimal/group separators, date-order expectations, measurement system, currency code, fraction digits, and rounding context. Keep the actual price's currency distinct from inferred regional defaults.

**Failure → avoidance:** reading `1.234,56` as `1.23456`, interpreting `03/04` incorrectly, assuming `$` means USD, or applying two decimals to every currency. Parse with locale-aware libraries, preserve source strings, use decimal arithmetic for money, and request clarification when the underlying input remains ambiguous.

**Best source:** [Unicode CLDR releases](https://cldr.unicode.org/index/downloads), including [CLDR JSON data](https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr-json) and [machine-readable currency metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/unicode-org/cldr-json/main/cldr-json/cldr-core/supplemental/currencyData.json). No account/key required. The user supplies their actual preferences; CLDR does not know them.

**Licence:** **Unicode-3.0**. Permission includes “**the rights to use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, and/or sell copies of the Data Files or Software**” ([licence](https://www.unicode.org/license.txt)). Preserve the copyright/permission notice in copies or associated documentation. This also covers the Unicode data supplement in item 5, subject to file-specific exceptions. Pin a release; refresh when preferences or releases change.

### 18. Place identity, coordinates, and administrative context

**Want:** stable place ID, normalized address, country/admin hierarchy, latitude/longitude with coordinate order/CRS, bounding box, candidate alternatives, and match confidence. Use the user's intended place rather than server-IP location.

**Failure → avoidance:** booking in the wrong London, mixing longitude and latitude, or assuming an incomplete map tag proves a business is open. Present/resolve ambiguous candidates; use publisher/merchant confirmation for live hours, inventory, and service eligibility.

**Best reusable source for this product:** [OpenStreetMap downloadable PBF data](https://planet.openstreetmap.org/pbf/), with a locally operated geocoder/index. Downloading requires no account/key or data fee; running an index has infrastructure costs. For a limited direct end-user lookup, [public Nominatim search JSON](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=London%2C%20UK&format=jsonv2&addressdetails=1) is also anonymous, but its **[usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/)** is essential: at most one request/second across the application, identifying headers, caching, no autocomplete/systematic harvesting, and no confidential inputs.

**Product constraint:** that policy expressly prohibits reselling geocoding results and requires geocoding-focused services to run their own instance. It also restricts generic integration through no-code/low-code/vibe-coding platforms and requires a deliberate developer decision. A commercial homepage must not simply proxy the public endpoint as a place-lookup product, even at low volume. Prefer the open downloads plus your own service; no production-ready, globally complete, zero-infrastructure-cost hosted alternative was established here.

**Licence:** **ODbL-1.0**. “**You are free to copy, distribute, transmit and adapt our data, as long as you credit OpenStreetMap and its contributors.**” ([copyright/licence](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)). Attribution and applicable database share-alike/source obligations matter for a commercial aggregate. An open-data licence does not remove public-server limits.

### 19. Entity resolution and dated relationships

**Want:** stable entity ID, type, aliases, official-website candidates, jurisdiction, external IDs, statement references/rank, and validity qualifiers. Distinguish discovery from verified present-day authority.

**Failure → avoidance:** confusing similarly named companies or relying on an obsolete office-holder. Match multiple identifiers, retain qualifiers/references, and verify current consequential claims against the relevant primary source. Never treat a community-edited website claim as an authentication allowlist without validation.

**Best source:** [Wikidata entity JSON](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:EntityData/Q84.json) and [documented data-access methods](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Data_access). Public reads require no account. Fetch bounded entities and cache revisions; public query services have service limits and should not carry an unbounded fan-out per homepage call.

**Licence:** **CC0** for the structured namespaces, not every page or linked asset: “**All structured data from the main, Property, Lexeme, and EntitySchema namespaces is available under the Creative Commons CC0 License**” ([policy](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Copyright)). Commercial reuse is permitted; linked sites/images have their own rights. Refresh according to property volatility.

### 20. Public holidays and business-day jurisdiction

**Want:** named jurisdiction/subdivision, dates, holiday names, substitute days, feed coverage horizon, and distinction between public holiday, bank settlement day, and an individual business's opening hours.

**Failure → avoidance:** promising a UK bank-day action on a Scottish-only holiday or assuming every organization closes on a public holiday. Select the actual region and relevant institution's calendar. [GOV.UK's bank-holiday page](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays) distinguishes the UK divisions and notes employment-related caveats.

**Best source for the UK:** [GOV.UK bank-holidays JSON](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays.json), anonymous and free. It exposes separate England/Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland event collections. It is not a global calendar or a business-hours database; outside the UK, prefer the relevant government's documented open feed and verify its licence separately.

**Licence:** **OGL-3.0**. GOV.UK states: “**All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated**” ([terms](https://www.gov.uk/help/terms-conditions)). The [OGL text](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/) provides commercial reuse subject to attribution and exceptions; direct licence retrieval was blocked in this research tool, while GOV.UK's licence declaration was readable. Suggested daily refresh plus handling exceptional announcements and coverage limits.

### 21. Exchange-rate reference with base, quote, and date

**Want:** base currency, quote currency, rate convention, observation date, publication/retrieval times, and indication that the rate is reference rather than executable. Keep taxes, merchant fees, and card spreads separate.

**Failure → avoidance:** reversing a rate, using a Friday rate as a live Monday quote, or promising a checkout total from a reference conversion. Require dated decimal values and the actual merchant/payment quote before spending.

**Best source:** [ECB daily reference-rate XML](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-daily.xml); [publisher documentation](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html). No account/key/fee. ECB normally publishes on working days around 16:00 CET, except TARGET closing days, and discourages transaction use. Coverage is a limited currency set with EUR as base.

**Commercial permission:** custom terms: “**users of this website may make free use of the information obtained directly from it**” ([ECB copyright conditions](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/services/using-our-site/disclaimer/html/index.en.html)). Credit ECB, reproduce accurately, identify modifications, and disclose free availability when incorporated into sold documents, as the terms specify. Fetch after publication; preserve the actual observation date over weekends/holidays.

### 22. Weather alerts, forecast validity, and affected geography

**Want:** official alert ID, issuing authority, affected polygon/zones, severity, onset/effective/expiry times, cancellations, forecast issue time, and explicit units. Include only when travel, delivery, outdoor work, or another physical-world task needs it.

**Failure → avoidance:** recommending a route or event under an active warning, using a cancelled alert, or applying an alert to the wrong location. Match time and geography; retain official links and check nearer the real-world action.

**Best qualifying source for US coverage:** [NWS active alerts JSON](https://api.weather.gov/alerts/active) and location-based forecasts via `/points/{lat},{lon}`, described in [NWS API documentation](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api). Free/no account/key; send an identifying User-Agent and follow caching/rate guidance. No claim of global coverage. Outside its coverage, use jurisdiction-specific official feeds with separately checked terms.

**Licence:** public domain unless noted otherwise. NWS says its information “**may be used without charge for any lawful purpose**” ([use policy](https://www.weather.gov/disclaimer)). Do not imply endorsement, claim ownership, or present modified information as official. Preserve expiry and cache headers; a stale or failed alert fetch means unknown, not no warnings.

### 23. Vulnerabilities matched to exact software versions

**Want:** ecosystem/package/version or commit, advisory IDs and aliases, affected ranges, fixes, withdrawn/modified dates, and source licence. Only activate for installs, code execution, dependency selection, or software-administration tasks.

**Failure → avoidance:** installing a known vulnerable dependency or assuming “no CVEs found” means safe. Match the exact ecosystem/version; consider withdrawn records, aliases, local inventory, and scan coverage. Do not infer a live website's backend package inventory from appearance alone.

**Best source:** [OSV JSON API](https://google.github.io/osv.dev/api/), including anonymous `POST https://api.osv.dev/v1/query` with package/version and `GET /v1/vulns/{id}`. Its [data-source documentation](https://google.github.io/osv.dev/data/) explicitly identifies upstream licences. An empty result only means no matched known advisory in that coverage.

**Licence:** **mixed**, not “OSV is Apache so all data is Apache.” Examples listed by OSV are GitHub/PyPI/Go under CC-BY-4.0, RustSec under CC0, and Ubuntu under CC-BY-SA-4.0. For the GitHub subset, the [upstream data licence](https://github.com/github/advisory-database/blob/main/LICENSE.md) is CC-BY-4.0; it grants rights to “**reproduce and Share the Licensed Material, in whole or in part**”. Commercial reuse is allowed subject to its conditions. Preserve per-record provenance/attribution and audit each additional upstream licence; mark unestablished mappings **unclear**. Refresh before installation and after new advisories.

### 24. Supported versions, deprecation, and end-of-life

**Want:** product identifier, release line, latest release, release date, support/security/end-of-life dates, extended-support conditions, and upstream references. Distinguish the installed version from the most recent available one.

**Failure → avoidance:** following an old tutorial into an unsupported runtime, interpreting “latest” as universally compatible, or recommending an upgrade without checking the support branch. Match the actual product/release and verify the vendor's policy for consequential changes.

**Best source:** [endoflife.date API v1 documentation](https://endoflife.date/docs/api/v1/) and [Python product JSON example](https://endoflife.date/api/v1/products/python). Free/no account/key. It is a community-maintained index rather than a contractual statement from every vendor; retain source links and missing-data state.

**Licence:** **MIT**. The [project licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/endoflife-date/endoflife.date/master/LICENSE) includes “**the rights to use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense, and/or sell copies**”; retain copyright/permission notices. A [maintainer discussion explicitly applies MIT to the data](https://github.com/endoflife-date/endoflife.date/discussions/5254). Suggested daily refresh and recheck before selecting a release.

### 25. Task-relevant recent events and primary-source discovery

**Want:** narrowly scoped topic/place/entity, recent source URLs, discovery/seen time, publisher, deduplicated clusters, and relevance. Distinguish event time from publication and indexing time. Return a few leads, not an instruction-bearing digest of everything happening globally.

**Failure → avoidance:** using stale model knowledge about a recent disruption, treating syndicated copies as independent corroboration, or assuming a fresh headline proves the underlying event happened today. Use news discovery to find primary announcements and verify the claim there before acting.

**Best broad qualifying discovery source found:** [GDELT DOC API documentation](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/gdelt-doc-2-0-api-debuts/), with JSON ArtList queries such as `https://api.gdeltproject.org/api/v2/doc/doc?query=internet&mode=artlist&format=json&maxrecords=5&timespan=1d`. [Downloadable GDELT datasets](https://gdeltproject.org/data.html) are another no-account option. Neither is a truth service, complete news index, or SLA-backed dependency. Prefer the relevant official RSS/Atom/JSON feed once the target authority is known.

**Commercial permission:** GDELT allows “**unlimited and unrestricted use for any academic, commercial, or governmental use of any kind without fee**” ([terms](https://gdeltproject.org/about.html#termsofuse)). Its terms require GDELT citation and a link when using or redistributing its datasets. This does **not** license publishers' linked articles, thumbnails, or full text; rights for those are **unclear unless separately granted**. Suggested topic-triggered refresh and bounded results; broad news should not be in every core response.

## What one JSON call should look like

**One call from the agent** can aggregate cached reference data, local context, and selected target observations. It does not mean one upstream fetch, nor that the homepage can know every future page, account state, or transaction outcome. Use it before the task; continue checking changing preconditions at action time.

The caller must supply enough context to select useful data. If it has no target yet, return the core and explicit `not_applicable`/`unknown` entries; do not probe arbitrary sites. The following is an **illustrative contract, not live findings**:

```json
{
  "request": {
    "task_id": "task-example",
    "intent": "research",
    "target_urls": ["https://example.com/"],
    "context_ref": "local://task-example",
    "locale": "en-GB",
    "timezone": "Europe/London",
    "modules": ["core", "health"]
  },
  "response": {
    "schema_version": "1.0",
    "generated_at": "2026-10-16T12:00:00Z",
    "task_id": "task-example",
    "context_version": "example-1",
    "authority": {
      "source": "runtime",
      "allowed_actions": ["read"],
      "external_writes_authorized": false,
      "spend_limit": {"amount": "0", "currency": "GBP"}
    },
    "clock": {
      "now_utc": "2026-10-16T12:00:00Z",
      "timezone": "Europe/London",
      "local_datetime": "2026-10-16T13:00:00+01:00"
    },
    "observations": [
      {
        "item_rank": 14,
        "key": "health",
        "subject": "https://example.com/",
        "state": "unavailable",
        "value": null,
        "source_url": null,
        "source_kind": "publisher",
        "source_updated_at": null,
        "observed_at": "2026-10-16T12:00:00Z",
        "expires_at": null,
        "coverage": {"components": [], "complete": false},
        "licence": {
          "id": null,
          "commercial_reuse": "unclear",
          "url": null,
          "quote": null,
          "attribution": null
        },
        "untrusted_text": true,
        "error": {"code": "no_official_feed_known", "retry_after": null}
      }
    ],
    "decision": {
      "status": "proceed_with_checks",
      "permitted_action_class": "read",
      "unresolved": ["No official service-health feed is known"],
      "recheck_before_write": ["authority", "session", "origins", "actions", "page"]
    }
  }
}
```

For observations, use explicit states such as `ok`, `partial`, `stale`, `unavailable`, `not_applicable`, and `not_checked`. `null` plus an error is different from an empty but complete result. Likewise, `not_listed` in a threat feed is different from a verified safe destination. Let policy decide whether a missing datum blocks the particular action: missing weather need not block web research; unknown transaction outcome should block an automatic repeat purchase.

**Implementation priorities:**

1. Build the local core first: task authority, time, capabilities, session references, action ledger, and source/coverage envelopes. These address failures a public feed cannot solve.
2. Add narrowly scoped target adapters for origin, robots, API discovery, HTTP limits, browser observations, and official status feeds. Enforce SSRF controls and policy even during preflight fetching.
3. Pre-cache open reference datasets and attach only relevant slices: timezone rules, PSL, Unicode/CLDR, and selected entity/place data. Include dataset version, licence, and attribution in the cache record.
4. Make holidays, FX, weather, vulnerabilities, lifecycle, and recent events opt-in by task relevance. Avoid charging every call the latency, privacy exposure, and token cost of every module.
5. Keep sensitive context local. A public no-account aggregator should accept coarse task/target inputs and cannot honestly return private authority, authenticated session, or receipt facts. A runtime-side merger can combine its public response with private local facts.
6. Give the response a bounded size/deadline and return partial results with reasons. Authenticate the trusted local policy channel; validate schema and task/context binding. A signed external feed still contains untrusted content and cannot override user authority.

## Research checks and limits as of 2026-10-16

This report uses primary specifications, dataset publishers, official project repositories, and maintainer statements. I did not rely on API-directory claims or assume a repository licence applies to upstream content. “Best” means the strongest fit found under the stated constraints, not an exhaustive comparison of every provider.

| Check | Result in this session | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Web-tool retrieval of GOV.UK holiday JSON, PSL, GitHub status JSON, endoflife.date Python JSON, OSV advisory JSON, and CLDR currency JSON | Machine-readable content retrieved without supplying an account/key | Evidence of accessible interfaces through this research tool, not an independent production uptime/SLA test |
| ECB daily XML | Web tool reported unsupported `text/xml` | Publisher documents the XML interface; this tool did not parse it |
| NWS alert JSON, CERT Polska list JSON, and Wikidata entity JSON | Direct web-tool retrieval failed | Formats/no-account routes are supported by publisher documentation; endpoint operation was not independently verified here |
| National Archives OGL page | Direct retrieval failed/403; GOV.UK terms and footer readable | OGL assignment is evidenced by GOV.UK; the full licence text was not successfully fetched here |
| Direct terminal HTTP checks of twelve endpoints | All failed at local DNS resolution | No claim of end-to-end terminal validation; this is not evidence that twelve providers are down |
| Other named downloads/discovery paths | Documentation/reference review unless explicitly listed above | Validate access, schemas, freshness, and exact terms before production deployment |

Important source-selection exclusions:

- **URLhaus:** its [current Community API page](https://urlhaus.abuse.ch/api/) requires an Auth-Key and says commercial/for-profit needs may require a paid subscription. It does not satisfy the requested unconditional free/no-account commercial fit.
- **Generic weather APIs:** an open data licence does not establish that the hosted free service permits commercial use. NWS is the stronger fit within its geographic scope; there is no global-coverage claim here.
- **Global holidays, live merchant prices/inventory, general legal/compliance verdicts, logged-in account state, and universal page-safety scores:** no single authoritative worldwide free/no-account source was established. Return scope-limited facts or `unknown`; do not fabricate coverage.
- **Status feeds and target page metadata:** useful to consume operationally, but public readability alone does not establish a commercial redistribution licence. These remain clearly marked `unclear`.

The central product opportunity is a trustworthy account of **what the agent knows, what it is allowed to do, what is missing, and what must be rechecked**. Open public datasets enrich that account; they cannot replace the runtime's view of authority and effects.
